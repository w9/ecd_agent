PROTOCOL SYNOPSIS

Study Title: A Phase 2 Randomized Study of QM-2201 in Adults with Progressive Familial Intrahepatic Cholestasis Spectrum Disorders

Phase: Phase 2

Indication: Progressive familial intrahepatic cholestasis (PFIC) spectrum disorders, including PFIC types 1 through 3 and related bile salt export pump deficiency phenotypes. This indication does not appear in the accompanying site history or country-level reference tables.

QM-2201 is an investigational ileal bile acid transporter modulator evaluated for reduction of serum bile acids and improvement in pruritus severity over 24 weeks of treatment. Participants are randomized 1:1 to QM-2201 or placebo, stratified by PFIC genotype class. The primary endpoint is percent change from baseline in fasting serum bile acids at Week 24. Secondary endpoints include change in pruritus scores, growth parameters, and liver biochemistry.

The study plans to enroll 48 participants at specialized hepatology centers, with interest in sites across the United States, Germany, France, and Japan where rare cholestatic disease clinics exist.

Eligibility language states that participants must be between 12 and 65 years of age at the time of informed consent. A subsequent paragraph describing the adult efficacy population states that only participants 18 years of age or older will be included in the primary analysis set, while earlier screening language asserts that adolescents from age 12 are eligible for enrollment and dosing. These age statements are not reconciled within the synopsis. Participants must have a genetically confirmed PFIC-related diagnosis or a clinical diagnosis supported by liver histology when genetic testing is inconclusive. Elevated serum bile acids at screening and chronic pruritus for at least three months are required. Exclusion criteria include prior liver transplantation, decompensated cirrhosis, and use of other IBAT inhibitors within 90 days. Pregnancy and breastfeeding are exclusionary.

The synopsis later notes that pediatric participants younger than 18 years may enroll only at sites with pediatric hepatology capability, which conflicts with the adult-only primary analysis statement without clarifying whether adolescents contribute to secondary endpoints alone. Sites are asked to confirm access to genetic testing for ABCB11, ATP8B1, and ABCB4 variants, although turnaround expectations are not specified.

Treatment duration is 24 weeks with an optional 48-week open-label extension. Safety follow-up continues for four weeks after the last dose. The estimated recruitment period is 18 months from first site activation.
